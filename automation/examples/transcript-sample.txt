A pod is the smallest thing Kubernetes runs. People hear cluster and picture a room of servers. The thing you actually start is a pod.

A pod holds one or more containers that share a network address and can share a disk. When the pod dies, those containers die with it. That is the mechanism.

Docker builds the container. Kubernetes decides where the pod runs, and it starts a replacement when the first one fails. The replacement is a new pod, not a repaired one.

The mistake is treating the YAML as the understanding. If you cannot say what the pod shares, and what it does not share, the file is ahead of you.

The line I would keep: a pod is a cabin, not the ship.
